# What Proof Is Vodka? Vodka ABV & Proof Explained

Standard vodka is **80 proof**, which equals **40% alcohol by volume (ABV)**. In the United States, "proof" is simply double the ABV, so a vodka that is 40% alcohol is 80 proof. Most vodka sold worldwide sits right at this 80-proof mark, though some bottlings range higher — up to 100 proof (50% ABV) or beyond for overproof styles.

If you've ever stared at a label wondering what "80 proof" actually means for your glass, this guide explains proof, ABV, and how they connect — using a real example in [Touch Vodka](/products), which is bottled at a classic **80 proof (40% ABV)**.

## What Does "Proof" Mean?

Proof is a measure of how much alcohol a spirit contains. In the U.S. system, the rule is straightforward:

> **Proof = ABV × 2**

So a spirit that is 40% alcohol by volume is 80 proof. A spirit that is 50% ABV is 100 proof. The term dates back centuries to a method of "proving" a spirit's strength by testing whether gunpowder soaked in it would still ignite — but today it's just a simple doubling of the ABV figure.

### Proof vs. ABV: What's the Difference?

ABV and proof describe the same thing — the alcohol concentration — just on different scales. ABV is a percentage; U.S. proof is that percentage doubled.

| ABV | U.S. Proof |
|---|---|
| 30% | 60 proof |
| 35% | 70 proof |
| 40% | 80 proof |
| 45% | 90 proof |
| 50% | 100 proof |

Outside the United States, many countries simply use ABV and skip proof altogether. That's why a European label may just read "40% vol." while an American one says "80 proof" — both describe an identical spirit.

## What Proof Is Standard Vodka?

The overwhelming majority of vodka is **80 proof (40% ABV)**. This has become the global benchmark for a few practical reasons:

- **Regulation:** In the U.S., vodka must be bottled at a minimum of 40% ABV (80 proof) to be legally labeled vodka.
- **Balance:** At 40% ABV, vodka is strong enough to carry flavor and mix well, but smooth enough to enjoy.
- **Tradition:** 80 proof has been the accepted standard for premium and everyday vodka alike for generations.

### Do Any Vodkas Go Higher?

Yes. While 80 proof is standard, some vodkas are bottled stronger:

- **90 proof (45% ABV):** A slightly bolder pour found in some premium lines.
- **100 proof (50% ABV):** Common in "high-proof" vodkas designed for spirit-forward cocktails.
- **Overproof (above 100 proof):** Specialty bottlings, often used by bartenders for layering or flaming, though these are far less common.

Higher proof means more alcohol per ounce and a more intense burn, which is why the 80-proof standard remains the most versatile and widely loved.

## Does Higher Proof Mean Better Vodka?

Not necessarily. Proof tells you the alcohol content — not the quality. Smoothness comes from the base ingredients, the distillation, and the filtration, not from a higher number on the label.

## How Proof Affects Your Drink

Understanding proof helps you pour with intention, whether you're measuring calories or mixing a cocktail.

### Proof and Calories

Alcohol carries calories, so higher-proof spirits have more calories per ounce. A standard 1.5 oz pour of 80-proof vodka is roughly 95 calories, with no carbs or sugar. Importantly, in a mixed drink it's usually the **mixer that adds the calories and sugar, not the vodka**. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What proof is most vodka?

Most vodka is 80 proof, which equals 40% ABV. This is the legal minimum strength for vodka in the United States and the global standard for both everyday and premium bottlings, including Touch Vodka.

### Is 80 proof vodka strong?

At 80 proof (40% ABV), vodka is a standard-strength spirit — the same strength as most whiskey, rum, gin, and tequila. It's strong enough to mix well and sip neat, but it's the baseline rather than an unusually high-proof spirit.

### What is the difference between proof and ABV?

ABV (alcohol by volume) is the percentage of alcohol in a spirit, while U.S. proof is that percentage doubled. A vodka at 40% ABV is 80 proof. They measure the exact same thing on two different scales.

### Can vodka be higher than 80 proof?

Yes. While 80 proof is standard, some vodkas are bottled at 90 proof (45% ABV) or 100 proof (50% ABV), and a few overproof styles go even higher. These are stronger and more intense, but 80 proof remains the most popular and versatile.

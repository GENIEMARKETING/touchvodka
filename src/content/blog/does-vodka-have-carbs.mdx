# Does Vodka Have Carbs? The Keto Drinker's Guide

No, pure distilled vodka does not have any carbs. A standard pour of unflavored vodka contains zero grams of carbohydrates and zero grams of sugar, which makes it one of the most keto-friendly alcoholic drinks available. The carbs in a vodka cocktail come entirely from the mixer—not the spirit.

If you're following a low-carb or ketogenic diet, that's great news: vodka can fit comfortably into your plan as long as you choose your mixers wisely. Below we explain why distilled vodka is carb-free, how it affects ketosis, and how to build keto vodka drinks that won't blow your carb budget.

## Why Pure Vodka Has Zero Carbs

It seems counterintuitive that a spirit made from grain or potatoes would have no carbohydrates. The answer, once again, is distillation.

When vodka is produced, yeast ferments the sugars in the base ingredient and converts them into alcohol. The fermented liquid is then distilled—heated so the alcohol vaporizes and separates from everything else, including any leftover sugars and starches. What condenses into the bottle is essentially pure ethanol and water.

Because carbohydrates don't carry over during distillation, the finished spirit contains none. ## Is Vodka Keto-Friendly?

Yes—plain vodka is keto-friendly because it has zero carbs and won't directly knock you out of ketosis. It's one of the cleanest choices on a low-carb bar menu, alongside other unflavored distilled spirits.

There's an important caveat, though. Your body prioritizes metabolizing alcohol over burning fat, so while vodka won't add carbs, it can temporarily pause fat-burning while it's being processed. This is normal and applies to all alcohol. In moderation, plain vodka remains compatible with a ketogenic approach.

### How alcohol affects ketosis

- **Vodka adds no carbs**, so it won't spike blood sugar on its own.
- **Alcohol is metabolized first**, which can slow fat-burning briefly.
- **Tolerance often drops on keto**, so drinks may hit harder—pace yourself.
- **Mixers are the real risk**, not the spirit.

## The Mixers That Add Carbs (and the Ones That Don't)

Here's where keto drinkers get tripped up. The vodka is clean, but the mixer can carry a surprising carb load. Use this quick reference.

| Mixer | Carbs | Keto-friendly? |
|---|---|---|
| Soda water / club soda | 0 g | Yes |
| Diet tonic | ~0 g | Yes |
| Fresh lime or lemon juice (splash) | ~1 g | Yes |
| Regular tonic water | ~22 g per can | No |
| Orange juice | ~26 g per cup | No |
| Cranberry juice cocktail | ~30 g+ | No |
| Simple syrup (1 oz) | ~28 g | No |

The pattern is clear: anything sweet adds carbs fast. Regular tonic, fruit juices, and syrups can turn a zero-carb spirit into a 25-gram cocktail. Stick to soda water, a splash of fresh citrus, or sugar-free options.

## How to Build Keto Vodka Cocktails

Keeping a vodka drink keto is straightforward once you know the rules. Build around the spirit and add flavor without sugar.

- **Vodka soda with lime** — the gold standard, essentially zero carbs.
- **Vodka with sparkling water and muddled berries** — a few berries add minimal carbs and big flavor.
- **Vodka, soda, and fresh herbs** like mint or basil for aroma.
- **Sugar-free flavored seltzer** as a mixer for variety.

### Does flavored vodka have carbs?

It depends on the product. Pure flavored vodka where the flavor comes from natural essences after distillation is often still very low in carbs. However, some sweetened or "dessert" flavored vodkas add sugar, which introduces carbs. Always check the label on sweetened products if you're tracking carbs carefully.

## Vodka vs. Other Low-Carb Drinks

Not all alcohol is created equal on keto. Distilled spirits generally win because distillation removes carbs, while fermented drinks like beer and sweet wine retain them.

- **Vodka, gin, tequila, whiskey** — 0 g carbs (unflavored, distilled)
- **Dry wine** — ~2–4 g carbs per glass
- **Light beer** — ~3–6 g carbs
- **Regular beer** — ~12 g+ carbs
- **Sweet cocktails** — 20 g+ carbs

Among these, unflavored distilled spirits like vodka sit at the very top of the keto-friendly list. To understand why the distillation step is so effective at stripping carbs, our overview of [how vodka is made](/blog/how-is-vodka-made) walks through the full process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does vodka have carbs or sugar?

No. Pure distilled vodka contains zero grams of carbohydrates and zero grams of sugar. The sugars from the original grain or potato are converted to alcohol during fermentation and then removed during distillation, so the finished spirit—like Touch Vodka—has no carbs at all.

### Can I drink vodka on keto?

Yes, plain vodka is one of the most keto-friendly drinks because it has no carbs. Just be mindful that alcohol is metabolized before fat, which can briefly pause fat-burning, and that tolerance often drops on a low-carb diet. Stick to soda water and fresh citrus as mixers to keep the drink keto.

### Will vodka kick me out of ketosis?

Plain vodka won't add carbs or spike blood sugar, so it won't directly knock you out of ketosis. It can temporarily slow fat-burning while your body processes the alcohol, but this is true of all alcohol and resolves on its own. Sugary mixers, not the vodka, are what actually add carbs.

### Which vodka has the fewest carbs?

All unflavored, properly distilled vodkas have zero carbs, so there's no "lowest-carb" winner among them—they're all carb-free.

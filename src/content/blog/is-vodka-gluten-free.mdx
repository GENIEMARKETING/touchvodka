# Is Vodka Gluten Free? What Celiacs Need to Know

Yes, vodka is generally gluten free—even when it's made from wheat, barley, or rye. The distillation process separates pure alcohol from the original grain mash, and gluten proteins are too large to carry over into the finished spirit. For most people avoiding gluten, including those with celiac disease, properly distilled vodka is considered safe to drink.

That said, "gluten free" is a question worth unpacking, because the answer depends on how the vodka is made, whether anything is added after distillation, and how sensitive your own body is. Below, we explain exactly why distillation strips out gluten, which vodkas you can trust, and how a wheat-based spirit like Touch Vodka can still belong on a gluten-free bar cart.

## Why Distilled Vodka Is Gluten Free Even From Wheat

Gluten is a protein found in wheat, barley, and rye. The confusion around gluten-free vodka comes from the fact that many vodkas start with one of these grains. The key is what happens after fermentation.

During distillation, the fermented mash is heated until the alcohol vaporizes. That vapor rises, separates from the heavier solids, and condenses back into liquid. Gluten proteins are far too large and heavy to evaporate, so they stay behind in the leftover mash. What collects in the bottle is essentially pure ethanol and water—no protein, and therefore no gluten.

This is why agencies like the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau and most celiac organizations consider distilled spirits gluten free, regardless of the starting grain. The grain is simply the raw material for fermentation; it does not survive the journey into the glass.

This is where you need to read labels more carefully. Pure, unflavored vodka is the simplest case. Flavored vodkas add ingredients after distillation, and those additives are where any gluten risk could theoretically reappear—though reputable flavor infusions rarely contain gluten.

## Gluten-Free Vodka vs. Beer and Other Drinks

It helps to see where vodka sits relative to other popular drinks. Distillation is the differentiator.

| Drink | Typically gluten free? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Distilled vodka (any grain) | Yes | Distillation removes gluten proteins |
| Beer (standard) | No | Brewed from barley, not distilled |
| Wine | Yes | Made from grapes, no gluten grains |
| Whiskey (distilled) | Generally yes | Distilled from grain, like vodka |
| Hard seltzer | Usually yes | Often sugar- or grape-based |

The takeaway: beer is brewed and never distilled, so the gluten remains. Vodka, whiskey, and other distilled spirits start similarly but are purified through distillation. This is the single most important distinction for anyone shopping for a safe drink.

## Which Vodkas Are Safest for Celiac Disease?

If you have celiac disease or significant gluten sensitivity, here's how to choose with confidence.

- **Prioritize distilled, unflavored vodka.** This is the cleanest, most predictable option.
- **Look at the base ingredient if you're cautious.** Vodkas distilled from potatoes, corn, or grapes contain no gluten grain at all and can offer extra peace of mind.
- **Read flavored-vodka labels.** Additives introduced after distillation are the only realistic source of gluten in a finished vodka.
- **Listen to your own body.** A small minority of very sensitive individuals report reactions even to distilled grain spirits. If that's you, choose a non-grain base.

### Is wheat-based vodka risky for celiacs?

For the overwhelming majority of people, no. Major celiac organizations classify distilled spirits—including those made from wheat—as gluten free because the distillation removes the protein. Touch Vodka's winter-wheat base falls squarely into this category thanks to its tenfold distillation. If you have any personal history of reacting to distilled spirits, however, consult your physician and consider a potato- or grape-based alternative.

## How to Build a Gluten-Free Vodka Cocktail

A gluten-free spirit only stays gluten free if your mixers cooperate. The good news: most classic vodka mixers are naturally gluten free.

- **Soda water, tonic, and club soda** — gluten free
- **Fresh citrus juice** — gluten free
- **100% fruit juices** — gluten free
- **Most simple syrups** — gluten free

Watch out for pre-made cocktail mixes, certain bitters, and anything malt-based, which can sneak gluten back in. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is vodka gluten free if it's made from wheat?

Yes. Even wheat-based vodka is considered gluten free because distillation removes the gluten proteins. The wheat is only the starting material for fermentation; the gluten stays behind in the mash and never reaches the bottle. Touch Vodka is made from winter wheat and distilled ten times, which produces a clean, gluten-free spirit.

### Can people with celiac disease drink vodka?

Most people with celiac disease can safely drink distilled vodka, since major celiac organizations classify distilled spirits as gluten free. A very small number of highly sensitive individuals prefer vodka made from non-grain bases like potato or grape for extra assurance. If you have concerns, talk with your doctor about what's right for you.

### Does flavored vodka contain gluten?

Pure flavored vodka usually does not, but flavors and additives are introduced after distillation, so they're the only realistic place gluten could appear. Reputable brands rarely use gluten-containing additives, but you should always check the ingredient statement on flavored products if you're highly sensitive.

### Is gluten-free vodka different from regular vodka?

Functionally, most regular distilled vodka is already gluten free, so there's often no difference at all. Some brands market "gluten-free vodka" using non-grain bases like potato or corn for shoppers who want extra reassurance. Standard 80-proof distilled vodka, like Touch, fits a gluten-free lifestyle without needing a special label.

# What Is Vodka Made From? Ingredients and Process

Vodka is made from a fermentable base—most commonly grain such as wheat, rye, or corn—combined with water. Yeast ferments the sugars in that base into alcohol, and the liquid is then distilled and filtered into the clean, neutral spirit we know as vodka. The two essential ingredients in the finished bottle are alcohol and water; everything else is removed along the way.

While grain is the classic starting point, vodka can also be made from potatoes, grapes, sugar beets, or even corn. What unites them all is the process: ferment, distill, filter. Below we break down each ingredient, walk through how vodka is made step by step, and show how a winter-wheat spirit like Touch Vodka comes together.

## The Core Ingredients in Vodka

At its simplest, vodka requires only two things in the bottle—but it starts with a few more on the production floor.

### 1. A fermentable base

This is the source of the sugars that become alcohol. The most common bases are:

- **Grain** (wheat, rye, corn) — produces clean, smooth spirits and is the most popular choice
- **Potatoes** — yield a fuller, slightly creamy mouthfeel
- **Grapes** — used in some premium vodkas for a softer profile
- **Sugar beets / molasses** — economical and common in some regions

Touch Vodka is made from **winter wheat**, a grain prized for the smooth, gentle character it lends to the finished spirit. You can learn more about that choice and the craft behind it through [our distillation process](/our-story).

### 2. Water

Water makes up most of the final volume and matters more than people realize. The mineral content and purity of the water shape the final taste and mouthfeel. Touch Vodka is blended with **mineral-rich spring water**, which contributes to its clean, balanced finish.

### 3. Yeast

Yeast is the engine of fermentation. It consumes the sugars in the base and converts them into alcohol and carbon dioxide. Yeast doesn't end up in the bottle, but without it there would be no alcohol to distill.

## How Vodka Is Made: Step by Step

The journey from grain to glass follows four main stages. Each one refines the spirit and strips away anything that isn't clean alcohol or water.

1. **Mashing.** The base ingredient is cooked and broken down so its starches convert into fermentable sugars.
2. **Fermentation.** Yeast is added, and over several days it transforms those sugars into alcohol, producing a low-strength "wash."
3. **Distillation.** The wash is heated so the alcohol vaporizes, separates from water and impurities, and condenses back into a much purer liquid. Repeating this concentrates and cleans the spirit.
4. **Filtration and dilution.** The high-proof spirit is filtered—often through charcoal—then blended with water down to bottling strength.

For a deeper, dedicated walkthrough of these stages, see our full explainer on [how vodka is made](/blog/how-is-vodka-made).

### How distillation count affects quality

Each distillation pass removes more impurities and produces a smoother spirit. Touch Vodka is distilled **ten times**, which is on the high end and yields an exceptionally clean base before filtration. After distillation, it's **charcoal-filtered** to polish the spirit further, then bottled at **80 proof (40% ABV)**—the standard strength for vodka.

## Does the Base Ingredient Change the Taste?

Yes, subtly. Although vodka is prized for being neutral, the base ingredient and water leave a faint signature in the finished spirit.

| Base ingredient | Typical character |
|---|---|
| Wheat | Smooth, soft, slightly sweet |
| Rye | Spicy, peppery edge |
| Corn | Light, gently sweet, very neutral |
| Potato | Full-bodied, creamy mouthfeel |
| Grape | Delicate, fruity softness |

Touch Vodka's winter-wheat base sits in the smooth, soft camp—part of why it pours so easily neat or in cocktails. This is also why the spirit is naturally free of gluten and carbs: distillation removes those compounds regardless of the grain. ## What Vodka Is *Not* Made With

Part of what defines vodka is its purity. A properly distilled vodka does not contain:

- **Carbohydrates or sugar** — removed during distillation
- **Gluten proteins** — too large to carry over from grain
- **Added color or flavor** (in unflavored vodka) — it should be crystal clear

Because of this neutrality, plain vodka is also relatively light—about 96 calories per 1.5-ounce shot, all from alcohol. For more on that, see our breakdown of [how many calories in vodka](/blog/how-many-calories-in-vodka).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is vodka traditionally made from?

Traditionally, vodka is made from grain—most often wheat, rye, or corn—or from potatoes, combined with water. Yeast ferments the sugars in the base into alcohol, which is then distilled and filtered. Touch Vodka uses winter wheat as its base for a smooth, soft character.

### Is vodka made from potatoes or wheat?

It can be either. Both are common bases, but most vodka today is grain-based because grain produces a clean, smooth spirit efficiently. Potato vodka is less common and tends to have a fuller, creamier mouthfeel. Touch Vodka is made from winter wheat.

### How is vodka made step by step?

Vodka is made in four stages: mashing the base ingredient to release sugars, fermenting those sugars into alcohol with yeast, distilling the liquid to separate and purify the alcohol, then filtering and diluting it with water to bottling strength. Touch Vodka is distilled ten times and charcoal-filtered before being bottled at 80 proof.

### Why is vodka considered a neutral spirit?

Vodka is called neutral because distillation and filtration remove most of the flavor compounds, sugars, and impurities from the base ingredient, leaving a clean spirit that's mostly alcohol and water. Subtle differences from the grain and water remain, but vodka is intentionally smooth and unobtrusive—ideal for sipping or mixing.

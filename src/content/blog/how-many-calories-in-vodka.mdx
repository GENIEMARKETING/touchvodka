# How Many Calories Are in Vodka? A Clear Breakdown

A standard 1.5-ounce shot of 80-proof vodka contains roughly 96 calories. All of those calories come from alcohol itself—pure distilled vodka has zero grams of carbohydrates, fat, or sugar. That makes vodka one of the leaner spirits when you drink it neat, on the rocks, or with a calorie-free mixer.

Of course, the number on your glass can climb fast once you add juice, soda, or syrup. In this guide we break down vodka calories by serving size and proof, compare vodka to other drinks, and show you how to keep a cocktail light without sacrificing flavor.

## Calories in Vodka by Serving Size

The calorie count depends almost entirely on how much you pour and the proof. Because alcohol carries about 7 calories per gram, higher-proof or larger pours mean more calories—nothing else is contributing.

| Serving | Approx. calories (80 proof) |
|---|---|
| 1 oz | ~64 |
| 1.5 oz (standard shot) | ~96 |
| 2 oz (double) | ~128 |
| 750 ml bottle | ~1,600 |

These figures assume standard 80-proof vodka, which is 40% alcohol by volume. Touch Vodka is bottled at 80 proof (40% ABV), so a 1.5-ounce pour lands right around that 96-calorie mark. You can see the full lineup in the [Touch Vodka collection](/products).

### Does proof change the calorie count?

Yes. Higher-proof vodkas pack more alcohol into the same volume, so they carry more calories per ounce. A 100-proof vodka has roughly 124 calories in a 1.5-ounce shot versus 96 for 80 proof. Since standard vodka—including every Touch expression—is 80 proof, the 96-calorie estimate is the most useful reference point for most drinkers.

## Why Vodka Has Zero Carbs and Sugar

This surprises a lot of people: a spirit made from grain has no carbs in the bottle. The reason is distillation. During fermentation, yeast converts the sugars in the grain into alcohol. Distillation then separates that alcohol from everything else—including residual sugars and carbohydrates.

What ends up in the bottle is essentially ethanol and water. There's no sugar, no starch, and no carbohydrate left to count. ## Vodka Calories vs. Other Drinks

Compared to beer, wine, and sugary mixed drinks, a plain pour of vodka is genuinely light. The difference usually comes down to carbohydrates and added sugar, which vodka simply doesn't have.

| Drink | Typical serving | Approx. calories |
|---|---|---|
| Vodka (neat) | 1.5 oz | ~96 |
| Light beer | 12 oz | ~100 |
| Regular beer | 12 oz | ~150 |
| Red wine | 5 oz | ~125 |
| Margarita | 8 oz | ~300+ |
| Vodka soda | varies | ~96 |

A vodka soda is one of the lowest-calorie cocktails you can order because the soda water adds essentially nothing. The trouble starts with juices, tonic (which contains sugar), and pre-made mixes.

## How to Build Low-Calorie Vodka Cocktails

You don't have to drink vodka straight to keep things light. A few smart swaps keep the calorie count close to that 96-calorie base.

- **Use soda water instead of tonic.** Tonic water has added sugar; club soda has none.
- **Add fresh citrus, not juice.** A squeeze of lime or lemon brings flavor with almost no calories.
- **Skip simple syrup** or use a sugar-free alternative.
- **Muddle fresh herbs** like mint or basil for aroma without sugar.
- **Watch portion creep**—a heavy hand on the pour is the most common hidden calorie source.

### A note on mixers and "empty calories"

Alcohol calories are sometimes called "empty" because they don't deliver protein, vitamins, or fiber. That doesn't make vodka unhealthy in moderation—it just means the calories add up without making you feel full. Pairing drinks with food and water helps you pace yourself.

## Does Vodka Make You Gain Weight?

Vodka itself isn't uniquely fattening, but alcohol calories still count toward your daily total. Because vodka has zero carbs and sugar, a neat pour or a vodka soda is among the lighter choices. Weight gain associated with drinking usually traces back to two things: sugary mixers and the late-night snacking that can follow a few rounds.

If you're calorie-conscious, the practical strategy is simple—choose plain or soda-based drinks, mind your pour size, and stay aware of how many you have. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many calories are in a shot of vodka?

A standard 1.5-ounce shot of 80-proof vodka contains about 96 calories. Every one of those calories comes from alcohol, since pure vodka has no carbohydrates, fat, or sugar. A larger or higher-proof pour will raise the count proportionally.

### Does vodka have sugar or carbs?

No. Pure distilled vodka, including Touch Vodka, contains zero grams of sugar and zero carbohydrates. The sugars from the original grain are converted to alcohol during fermentation and then separated out during distillation, leaving only ethanol and water in the bottle.

### What is the lowest-calorie way to drink vodka?

Vodka neat, on the rocks, or with soda water and a squeeze of citrus is the lowest-calorie way to drink it—right around 96 calories per shot. Avoid tonic, juice, and pre-made mixes, which add sugar and significantly raise the total calorie count of the drink.

### Is vodka less fattening than beer or wine?

Per standard serving, a neat pour of vodka (~96 calories) is comparable to or lighter than a glass of wine (~125 calories) and a regular beer (~150 calories), largely because vodka has no carbohydrates. How you mix it matters most—sugary cocktails can easily exceed all of these.

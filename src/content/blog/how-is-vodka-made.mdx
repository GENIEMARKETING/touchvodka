# How Is Vodka Made? From Grain to Glass, Explained

Vodka is made in four core steps: a grain (or other starch source) is fermented into a low-alcohol wash, that wash is distilled one or more times to concentrate and purify the alcohol, the spirit is filtered to remove impurities, and finally it is cut with water down to bottling strength, typically 40% alcohol by volume. Each step shapes the smoothness and character of the finished spirit, which is why two vodkas made from the same grain can taste worlds apart.

At [Touch Vodka](/products), crafted in Tampa, Florida since 2012, that process is taken to an extreme: our winter wheat base is distilled **10 times**, charcoal-filtered, and blended with mineral-rich spring water before reaching **80 proof (40% ABV)**. Below, we break down every stage so you understand exactly what's in your glass.

## The Four Stages of Making Vodka

At its simplest, every bottle of vodka travels the same path from raw ingredient to finished spirit. Understanding these stages helps explain why some vodkas taste clean and silky while others feel sharp and harsh.

1. **Fermentation** — sugars in the base ingredient are converted to alcohol by yeast.
2. **Distillation** — the fermented wash is heated and condensed to concentrate the alcohol.
3. **Filtration** — the spirit is purified, often through charcoal, to strip away off-notes.
4. **Dilution and bottling** — water is added to bring the spirit to its final proof.

Here's how each stage works in detail.

## Step 1: Choosing and Fermenting the Base Ingredient

Vodka starts with a fermentable source of starch or sugar. Traditionally that meant grains like wheat, rye, or corn, but potatoes, grapes, and even sugar beets can all become vodka. The base ingredient matters because it leaves a subtle fingerprint on the final flavor and mouthfeel.

### Why the Base Grain Matters

Wheat-based vodkas — like [Touch Artisan](/products/touch-artisan) — tend to be smooth with a gentle, slightly sweet finish. Rye lends a peppery edge, corn produces a softer body, and potato vodka is often creamy and full. We use **winter wheat**, a hardy grain harvested after a cold dormant season, because it ferments into an exceptionally clean, mellow spirit.

### How Fermentation Works

Once the grain is milled and cooked into a mash, enzymes convert its starches into fermentable sugars. Yeast is then added, and over the next few days it consumes those sugars and produces alcohol and carbon dioxide. The result is a "wash" — a beer-like liquid usually around 7 to 12% ABV. This wash is the raw material every subsequent step refines.

## Step 2: Distillation — The Heart of the Process

Distillation is where vodka truly takes shape. By heating the fermented wash, distillers separate alcohol (which boils at a lower temperature than water) from water and unwanted compounds, then condense the alcohol vapor back into a more concentrated, purer liquid.

### Pot Stills vs. Column Stills

There are two main approaches:

- **Pot stills** process one batch at a time and tend to retain more of the base ingredient's character.
- **Column stills** run continuously and can reach very high purity in a single tall column, which is why most modern vodka relies on them.

### How Many Times Is Vodka Distilled?

This is one of the most asked questions about vodka, and the answer varies widely. Many vodkas are distilled two or three times. Premium brands push further. We distill Touch **10 times**, progressively stripping away congeners — the trace compounds responsible for harshness — until what remains is remarkably soft and neutral. You can read more about [our distillation process](/our-story) and why we don't cut corners on this step.

It's worth noting that beyond a certain point, additional distillations offer diminishing returns. The number alone doesn't guarantee quality — technique, equipment, and the base ingredients all matter just as much.

## Step 3: Filtration — Polishing the Spirit

After distillation, vodka is often filtered to remove any lingering impurities and smooth out the texture. The most common method is **charcoal (activated carbon) filtration**, where the spirit passes through layers of carbon that trap unwanted molecules.

### Common Filtration Methods

| Method | What It Does |
|---|---|
| Charcoal / activated carbon | Removes off-flavors and harsh congeners |
| Quartz or sand | Provides gentle mechanical polishing |
| Cellulose membranes | Filters out fine particles for clarity |

We charcoal-filter Touch after distillation to ensure every expression — from [Touch Key Lime](/products/touch-key-lime) to [Touch Ruby](/products/touch-ruby) — pours crystal clear and tastes clean from the first sip. Filtration is the final polish before the spirit is brought down to drinking strength.

## Step 4: Dilution, Proofing, and Bottling

Freshly distilled vodka is far too strong to drink — often 90% ABV or higher. To make it palatable, distillers cut it with water until it reaches its target proof. By law in the United States, vodka must be bottled at a minimum of 40% ABV (80 proof).

### Why Water Quality Matters

Because water can make up more than half the volume of the finished bottle, its quality is critical. Hard, mineral-heavy, or chlorinated water can introduce off-flavors. We blend Touch with **mineral-rich spring water**, which contributes to its signature soft, rounded mouthfeel. Once proofed to **80 proof (40% ABV)**, the vodka is filtered one last time, bottled, sealed, and ready to ship.

## So, How Is Vodka Made? A Quick Recap

To summarize the grain-to-glass journey:

- **Ferment** a base grain (Touch uses winter wheat) into a low-alcohol wash.
- **Distill** the wash repeatedly to concentrate and purify the alcohol (Touch is distilled 10 times).
- **Filter** the spirit through charcoal to polish away harshness.
- **Dilute** with quality water to 40% ABV and bottle.

The same blueprint produces everything from bottom-shelf bottles to ultra-premium spirits — the difference lies in the ingredients, the number of distillations, the filtration, and the water. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is vodka made from?

Vodka can be made from any fermentable starch or sugar source. The most common bases are grains such as wheat, rye, and corn, but potatoes, grapes, and sugar beets are also used. Touch Vodka is made from winter wheat. For a deeper dive, see our guide on [what vodka is made from](/blog/what-is-vodka-made-from).

### How many times is vodka distilled?

It depends on the brand. Many vodkas are distilled two or three times, while premium producers distill more. Touch Vodka is distilled 10 times to remove harsh congeners and achieve an exceptionally smooth profile. Beyond a point, extra distillations offer diminishing returns, so technique matters as much as the number.

### Is all vodka gluten-free?

Vodka distilled from gluten grains is generally considered gluten-free by most experts because distillation removes gluten proteins, though those with severe sensitivities sometimes prefer vodka from non-gluten bases. For the full explanation, read [is vodka gluten-free](/blog/is-vodka-gluten-free).

### What gives vodka its smoothness?

Smoothness comes from a combination of factors: a clean base ingredient, multiple distillations that strip away harsh congeners, careful charcoal filtration, and high-quality water for dilution. Touch combines all four — winter wheat, 10× distillation, charcoal filtration, and mineral-rich spring water.
